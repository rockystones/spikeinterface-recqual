Blackrock Utah array · hardware to data file

The electrode number is not the channel number

A 96-electrode Utah array records 96 channels, and almost none of them keep the same number along the way. Electrode 78 is channel 1. This page follows one signal through all three pieces of hardware that renumber it — click anything to trace a different one.

Array Go to

01 The array, seen from the pad side

A Utah array has 100 electrodes, of which 96 are connected to the pedestal connector[5] — so four shanks are physically present but wired to nothing, and never appear in the map file. Each square below is one connected shank; the number on it is its electrode id, the elecNN value the datasheet and map file give it.[4]

The colour is the amplifier bank that shank's wire reaches, and that is the point of this picture: the colours are scattered, not blocked. Neighbouring electrodes land in different banks, so the wire bundle thoroughly shuffles the order before anything is recorded. That shuffle is the whole reason a map file has to exist.

Which four shanks go unconnected varies from array to array. Blackrock states it plainly: “arrays are often highly customized and the exact channel mappings vary from device to device. Please refer to the mapping datasheet included with your array.”[4] Usually the four corners are left out; on SN 1025-004377 two corners are connected instead and two other positions are dropped — switch to implant 2 anterior above to see it. In this lab's experience that substitution is how a broken shank is compensated during manufacture, using a surviving one elsewhere to still reach 96.[8] Either way: never reuse one array's map for another.

02 The pedestal and connector

A CerePort array assembly is four things: the electrode array, the wire bundle, two reference wires, and the pedestal connector.[4] The bundle runs to that pedestal on the skull, whose contact pads are grouped into banks of 32 signal pins — “connector pins are associated into banks because most devices in the field process data in 32 channel banks/increments”[4] — and this is where bank + pin becomes the electrode's real address.

The map file's column called elec is the pin, not an electrode. Blackrock defines the five .cmp columns as: col and row, the position in Central's Spike Panel with 0 = left and 0 = bottom; bank, the channel's bank lettered A–H; elec, “the channel's Pin in its given Bank, numbered 1-32”; and label, the electrode's name.[3] The electrode number is in label, and nowhere else.

Each bank carries a reference and a ground alongside its 32 signals. On the amplifier side that makes a 34-pin bank: “The Front-End Amplifier has four 34-pin banks. Each bank consists of 32 channels, a bank reference pin, and a ground pin.”[1] An array's own datasheet may list a few more positions per block for those references and grounds; the number that matters downstream is always the 32 signal pins.

03 Amplifier, and the number in your file

The amplifier samples the banks in order, and the pins in order within each bank. That sampling order is the channel id — nothing else defines it.[2] Blackrock states the resulting numbering directly:

“By default (i.e., bank A on ICS connected to bank A of the amplifier, bank B of ICS connected to amplifier's bank B, et cetera), pins 1-32 on Bank A will be channels 1-32 in Central, pins 1-32 on Bank B will be channels 33-64 in Central, and pins 1-32 on bank C will be channels 65-96 in Central.” LB-0514 Rev 5.00, Blackrock Research Arrays IFU, p11 [4]

Note the words “by default”: this holds when each bank is connected straight through. An adapter that crosses banks would change it, and the same document warns that map files “assume analog configurations” — check with Blackrock before trusting a map through a custom adapter or a digital headstage.[4]

What the acquisition software shows you

Central's Hardware Configuration[1] lists every channel against its physical location. This table is a property of the amplifier — it is the same whichever array you plug in, and it never mentions electrodes. Central even offers to name channels by “terminal numbering … the pin and bank value of the channel location on the front-end amplifier.”[1] Note that Central numbers the banks 1–4 where the map file letters them A–D; the file spec confirms they are the same four (“Front-End Bank A, B, C, D are 1, 2, 3, 4”).[2]

The Spike Panel “displays detected spikes on the front-end amplifier channels in a grid-graph form” — amplifier channels, note, not electrodes.[1] Only when you “apply a map file to the display” does it “change the spatial arrangement of the channels on the screen to match the electrode array in use.”[1] Loading the map also relabels the tiles with their electrode numbers.[4]

This is where the mistake usually happens. The default Spike Panel is a 10×10 grid of neural-looking tiles, which reads as a picture of the array. It is a picture of the amplifier. Judging which part of cortex is active from an unmapped panel gets the geometry wrong in exactly the way the wire bundle scrambled it.

04 Then vs now: where part 2 ends and part 3 begins

The pedestal connector and the amplifier are separate objects. In the analog era the pedestal ran through a cable to the Front-End Amplifier's four 34-pin banks.[1] A digital headstage such as the CerePlex E now mounts on the pedestal itself and “converts analog signals to digital format at the recording site” before sending them on.[6] The packaging changed; the numbering did not, because the banks and pins did not.
