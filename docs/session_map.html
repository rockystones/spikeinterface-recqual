The drive problem was a working-directory coincidence

Kilosort4 was 0 of 43 on C: and 185 of 195 on D:, which looked like a drive capability. It was not Docker and not a mount — the container run succeeds. SI writes a drive-less path into the recording JSON (C:\MyData\… → /MyData/…), correct inside the container, and then re-reads it on the host, where Windows resolves a leading slash against the current drive. From the repo on D:, /MyData/… became D:\MyData\… and NEO raised after the sorter had done all its work.

Chdir to the recording's drive around the result load: C: went to 38 of 43, and Rocky's coverage from a two-month 2025 window to its full 2018–2025 span. Nothing was staged or copied.

Separately, of the ten non-Rocky failures whose logs survived, five are the geometry error and four are n_samples < n_clusters on near-empty channel groups.

Corrected: NEV recovery is not usable for any sorter

Striped surface treatments

done

Nigel's and Fisk's arrays are coated in alternating stripes, so each array contains its own control. No detectable effect on any metric, on any array, under 13 sorting methods.

serves G5G3
reasoning → stage 13 · The striped arrays
geometry · why the obvious test lied · sensitivity

Which way the stripes run — the whole analysis

The treatment schematic has the wire bundle at the bottom; Blackrock CMP files are written with it on the right. So the schematic is the CMP rotated 90° clockwise, and a horizontal stripe in the figure is a line of constant CMP column. Splitting on row — the obvious reading of "alternating rows" — would have been perpendicular to the treatment and returned a meaningless null.

The implant table is transposed

The schematic's two panels carry real electrode maps and match their arrays cell for cell, including unconnected positions. They identify the opposite arrays from the implant table. Confirmed: anterior is TNP. Fisk's half is carried across on the surgical design and remains inferred, not verified.

The obvious test fires — and so does its control

A per-session paired test finds 23 of 44 array-metrics significant, several at p < 0.0001. The same pipeline on row parity, where no treatment exists, finds 29 of 44. The control fires more often than the treatment axis.

Cause: the session is not the unit of replication. The same 96 electrodes are recorded every session, so a paired test asks whether two fixed electrode sets differ at all — which is essentially never exactly false. Correcting only for spatial gradients leaves it unchanged at 18/24 versus 18/24.

The valid test

Rank the real 5/5 stripe assignment against all C(10,5)=252 ways of splitting the same ten stripe means, which holds the array's spatial structure fixed. Treatment axis 0/168, control axis 0/168 across 13 methods — the automatic sort, two human curators, eight Plexon algorithm settings, and the sorting-free layer.

What it could have seen

Minimum detectable effect on yield is 28–69% depending on array; on the noise floor, 5–15%. So a large effect is excluded and a modest one is not. Yield is nominally lower on the L1 stripe on all four arrays, but the sign test gives p = 0.125 and the gradient-corrected estimator disagrees on direction between animals — treat the direction as unresolved.

Impedance, operators & measurement floors

done

Impedance is a weak, array-specific predictor of yield — never a classifier. And holding the operator fixed protects the level, not the slope.

serves G5G3
instability · what it predicts · the operator drift

A single reading cannot classify an electrode

Over 41 measurement dates the median channel crosses 1 MΩ seven times, and only about a fifth never cross. Use a per-channel median across dates; treat 1 MΩ as a reporting convention, not a diagnosis.

Grouped on channel-median impedance, high-impedance electrodes cross threshold at half the rate (13.7 → 7.0 Hz) but record at 95% of the good group's SNR. The 1 MΩ convention would condemn a third of the working electrodes.

Operator floor, 14 matched pairs

Sidd keeps fewer units than DS in 14 of 14 pairs — perfectly systematic — while the two partition the kept spikes almost identically (ARI 0.998). They disagree about inclusion, not about clustering.

The offset drifts, which revises an earlier conclusion

A constant operator offset cancels out of a within-operator trend; a drifting one does not. On Medial, Sidd's unit count declines significantly while DS's is flat; on Lateral, DS's rises while Sidd's is flat. Same array, same sessions, same events — opposite conclusions. Holding the operator fixed protects the level, not the slope.

Three formats, three silent unit errors

  • TDT snippets — float32 volts, needing ×1e6. NEO also reads two samples too many: the Tbk says 40 points, the tsq record says 38. Samples 38–39 are foreign bytes, and they were the source of the "0.2% NaN snippets".
  • TDT Raw — µV for Oops/Picasso but int16 ADC counts for Luigi's 2013 rig. The tell was 3.28e10 = 32767 × 1e6.
  • Plexon — wf_gain derives from SpikeMaxMagnitudeMV, so raw × gain is millivolts. Anchored by the noise floor: 12.5 µV against 12,520 µV.

Luigi's sorting-free layer: 5 of 193 → 175 of 175

NEO reads the whole tsq index with np.fromfile and immediately copies it again with np.hstack, so its peak is twice the file. Luigi's indices reach 1.1 GB. Removing the redundant copy halved it.

A memmap was tried for the same purpose and reverted: NEO masks over the whole index once per store-channel pair, so paging it turned the largest block into 608 s and RSS still reached 1.4 GB. All of the cost, none of the saving.

The 18 blocks not attempted have no live store — declared eNe stores that never acquired. Coverage is complete, not partial.

TDT channel map

negative result

Inference from Rocky's same-day Blackrock scored 0.021 against a calibration ceiling of 0.193–0.224. No map written — which blocks every spatial figure for the TDT animals.

servesG1
why the ceiling matters more than the score

You asked whether the comparison assumed neuronal activity is stable across sessions. It did not — it compared inferred maps across days. But it had never been calibrated: nobody knew what a working method scores on that test.

Adding the control fixed that. On 121 same-day analog/digital NEV pairs where the truth is known to be the identity map, within-day recovery is 0.448 against 0.010 chance, and cross-day agreement of within-day maps on that known map is 0.193–0.224. The TDT attempt scored 0.021 on all four pairings — far below what a method that works achieves on this very test.

Controls that carry the negative results

4

A null is only worth the control behind it. These are the four that make the "no effect" conclusions mean something.

row parity · permutation null · calibration ceiling · chance level
  • Row-parity control (surface) — an axis with no treatment on it. It fired more than the treatment axis under the naive test, which is what exposed the design flaw.
  • Permutation null (surface) — the real 5/5 stripe assignment ranked against all 252 alternatives, holding spatial structure fixed. 0/168 on both axes.
  • Calibration ceiling (channel map) — what a method that works scores on the same test. Without it, 0.021 is uninterpretable.
  • Chance level (NEV recovery) — shuffled spike times. It revealed that three of four sorters recover crossings at chance.

Pipeline bugs found & fixed

6

Three of them were the same mistake: a scoped run overwriting a whole-corpus artifact.

reasoning → stage 14 · Running it at corpus scale
the partial-overwrite family and the rest

Partial runs clobbering aggregates — three instances

  • Session shards — --sorters kilosort4 replaced a shard holding three CPU sorters with one KS4 row. This destroyed real results: the two sessions where KS4 first succeeded lost their other three sorters.
  • Sorter summary — rebuilt from the run's own frames, so a 2-session run left a 2-session corpus summary.
  • Cohort merge — --part tdt dropped every extension subject, deleting Chase.

The rule: anything written at the end of a run must merge with what is on disk, or be rebuilt from the per-item files. Treat every "write the aggregate at the end" line as guilty until checked.

Others

  • Orphaned worker pools — joblib pools survive a clean child exit. 18 workers held 30 GB of commit with no failure recorded anywhere. Reaping took commit 72% → 43%.
  • purge_work deleting the evidence — failure rows say "see <folder>" and that folder was being deleted. Two KS4 failures were recorded and their logs destroyed in the same pass. Fixing this is what later made the geometry diagnosis possible.
  • Kilosort4 on this GPU — three layered faults: a cu118 wheel with no sm_120 kernels, an SI ↔ KS4 API mismatch that passes the version guard, and a cross-drive Docker mount. All fixed; KS4 now runs 125/128 on Fisk.

Luigi's implant identity

needs you

Array2 is 144 blocks in 2013; Array1 is 31 blocks in 2015–16. They never overlap, and the registry's implant window excludes the 2013 half entirely.

why it changes the analysis

Oops and Picasso record both arrays simultaneously — that is what "two arrays, one implant" looks like. Luigi does not. If the 2013 recordings are a different implant, the two must not share an implant-age axis, and they are currently carried under one label because splitting would assert a second implant nobody has confirmed.

Related and separate: January 2013 is a step, not a starting point. Noise, amplitude and yield all shift together between January and February (noise 7.96 → 18.16 µV, amplitude 36 → 95 µV). Metrics moving together by a common factor is a gain or filter change, not tissue. Read Array2 from February, where yield falls 0.62 → 0.01 in three months.

Kilosort4 parameter sweep — go or no-go

needs you

A sweep over dminx and max_channel_distance against a fixed reference is what would settle whether the over-splitting is real.

scope and cost

Reference could be Plexon's own sort or agreement with the other three sorters. Kilosort4 reproduced 141 units exactly on a re-run, so it is deterministic here — a sweep would measure the parameter rather than run-to-run noise.

Until this is settled, every KS4 unit count in the corpus is conditional on dminx=32, and CLAUDE.md's over-splitting gotcha should not be cited as established.

done

The layer earns its place on its first pass. Eight sessions in Feb–Mar 2025 carry up to 76% of total LFP power at 60 Hz, against a two-year baseline that never exceeds 4.5%. Noise floor 11.1 → 20.8 µV, units per electrode 0.98 → 0.33. Without this layer that is end-of-life array failure.

two ways the code was wrong, both silent

The guard failed open

The band check was wrapped in except Exception: pass. A validity check wrapped in try/except-and-continue is not a check.

The filter margin was 2000× too small

SI's margin_ms defaults to 5 ms, sized for a 300 Hz spike-band corner. A 0.5 Hz high-pass has a ~0.32 s time constant, so every chunk boundary carried a transient. Measured decay of chunked-vs-one-shot error: 6.7e-3 at 3 s, 3.4e-4 at 6 s, 5.8e-6 at 10 s. Set to 10 s.

Any SI filter chain reaching below ~10 Hz needs this raised, and the failure is silent — the traces look plausible either way.

the chain, and what it is tested against

bandpass_filter(0.5, 250, margin_ms=10000) → decimate(30), 30 kHz to 1 kHz. SI's decimate is plain array slicing and does no anti-aliasing — its own docstring points at resample as the safe choice. It is safe here only because the 250 Hz corner already removed everything above the 500 Hz output Nyquist.

resample was considered and rejected: FFT-based, so it wants a whole segment in memory, and the anti-alias filter it needs is one this layer applies anyway. Filtering first makes the aliasing argument explicit rather than delegating it.

New tests/ directory. The aliasing invariant is tested against a control that runs decimate without the band-pass and requires the alias to actually appear — a test that cannot fail is worse than no test. Six tests, all passing.

the generalisation I drew from that cell was wrong

The obvious hypothesis — method disagreement grows as an array dies — tested against Rocky's 60 session×array cells (2017-09 to 2023-08, reaching down to 2 gate-passing units, and representative: median cohort yield 0.260 units/electrode in-sample against 0.271 out).

It fails, and the way it fails is worth more than the cell. max/min is the obvious spread measure, and on this data it rises with yield: ρ = +0.44, p = 0.001. That number is an artefact — max/min is undefined when a method reports zero, which happens in exactly the 7 cells where an array is dying. The test silently censored its own counterexamples.

The coefficient of variation is defined whenever the mean exceeds zero, keeps all 60 cells, and finds no relationship:

yield quartilecellsmedian yieldCVa method at 0
lowest153.00.6397
low1532.00.4780

highest1580.00.5960

ρ = −0.018, p = 0.89. Relative disagreement between clustering methods is a constant of the pipeline, near 0.5–0.6, not a function of array health. And Rocky never produces the Nigel pattern: zero of 60 cells have one method at 0 while another exceeds 10 — so that cell is an outlier against another animal's full measured range, not an instance of a rule.

Prefer a spread measure defined on the whole support. A ratio that is undefined at zero removes the observations that matter most, and the surviving correlation looks like a finding.
